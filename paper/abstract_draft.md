# Abstract Draft

Human-in-the-loop systems can preserve nominal choice even when a person's override no longer changes the next outcome. We frame effective override loss as an observability problem: the relevant control-loss signal may emerge across a process and be absent from the final visible state, making the failure architectural rather than merely a classifier weakness. We introduce Agency Trajectory Benchmark v0, a synthetic matched-control benchmark seed that marks the first step where an attempted or plausible override ceases to influence the next state because of system structure. Using one fixed LLM rater, we compare full-trajectory evaluation with a final-state snapshot baseline. In Experiment 1, containing 30 trajectories in 15 pairs, trajectory ratings achieved 1.000 agreement and 1.000 Cohen's kappa with authored labels, versus 0.760 covered-case agreement and 0.464 kappa for snapshots. In Experiment 2, containing 20 trajectories in 10 lexical hard-control pairs, trajectory ratings retained 0.950 agreement and 0.900 kappa, versus 0.722 covered-case agreement and 0.444 kappa for snapshots. Across both experiments, the final-step snapshot detected 9 of 25 positives and missed or withheld judgment on 16 of 25. A documented false positive illustrates the boundary between adverse outcome and ineffective override. The data are synthetic and author-generated, use no human annotators, and provide no real-world validation.
